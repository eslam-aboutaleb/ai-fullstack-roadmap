QUESTION 261Insurance code & design analysis#

261. Why is coverage support different from adjudicating a claim?

Read / reveal answer

Coverage support explains retrieved policy language and identifies missing facts. Adjudication applies an authorized business process to a specific claim and records a decision. A conversational assistant should not imply that an explanation is a binding approval or denial.

Mechanism & reason

The prompt asks for coverage procedures and source-grounded facts, while claim status transitions live in deterministic application code. Administrative transitions are currently denied by default through an empty actor allowlist.

Risk & safeguards

A fluent statement such as this is covered can be interpreted more strongly than the available evidence supports. The host must distinguish general sample-policy scope from verified personal contract applicability.

Choice & trade-offs

Careful conditional explanations may feel less decisive and preserve the actual authority boundary. Automated adjudication is a separate product requiring substantially stronger domain and governance evidence.

QUESTION 262Insurance code & design analysis#

262. How should incident date, filing date, and policy effective date be handled?

Read / reveal answer

They describe different events. Incident date concerns the reported occurrence, filing date records submission, and policy effective dates identify versions or applicability intervals. None should be guessed from another simply to fill a required field.

Mechanism & reason

Claims have a nullable incident_date and database timestamps. Preparation can carry incident date through pending JSONB, while PolicyVersion has effective_from and effective_to. Their presence does not prove the retrieval path joins them correctly.

Risk & safeguards

Applying today's policy to an earlier incident can produce an inaccurate answer. Time zones and inclusive/exclusive boundary rules also matter for exact applicability.

Choice & trade-offs

Explicit temporal modeling adds query and validation work. A current-only general FAQ is simpler if the UI honestly limits its scope.

QUESTION 263Insurance code & design analysis#

263. Why must exclusions and conditions be retrieved alongside a positive coverage clause?

Read / reveal answer

A positive clause can be limited by exclusions, definitions, sublimits, deductibles, and factual conditions elsewhere. Retrieving one semantically similar paragraph is not necessarily sufficient to explain applicability.

Mechanism & reason

The procedure-and-policy split is intended to guide a fuller review. Current query_policy returns five retrieved results and section-level citations; that implementation does not guarantee every related exception is included.

Risk & safeguards

A faithful summary of an isolated clause can still mislead by omitting a controlling exception. More top-k hits may not solve the problem if the corpus lacks structured relationships.

Choice & trade-offs

Targeted follow-up retrieval or structured links improve completeness and add calls and complexity. Large indiscriminate context increases noise and cost.

QUESTION 264Insurance code & design analysis#

264. Why is a section citation weaker than a revision-aware evidence reference?

Read / reveal answer

A section label identifies a human-readable location, but may not uniquely identify source, revision, or exact supporting span. A reproducible reference needs enough identity to retrieve what the user originally saw.

Mechanism & reason

query_policy deduplicates citations by section and returns source/section with optional distance. It does not currently expose the library's complete revision-aware EvidenceItem contract. Two sources with the same section name can therefore need richer identity.

Risk & safeguards

A later policy edit can make an old answer unverifiable, and section-only deduplication can collapse distinct sources. A fallback source filename is not evidence of the original document's identity.

Choice & trade-offs

Rich citations add metadata and snapshot retention while enabling verification. Simple labels work for a small sample corpus and should not be overstated.

QUESTION 265Insurance code & design analysis#

265. How should OCR, tables, photographs, and attachments extend the insurance pipeline?

Read / reveal answer

Treat each modality as a separate extraction and evidence problem. OCR converts visible text, tables need row-column relationships, and photographs may require a vision model or human review. A file upload alone does not create reliable multimodal reasoning.

Mechanism & reason

The current backend demonstrates text policy/procedure ingestion and structured claim descriptions. The Ragnor extractor seam can support extensions, but the inspected host does not establish a complete attachment-processing workflow.

Risk & safeguards

OCR errors in amounts, dates, or negations can change meaning. Attachments can also contain malware, private third-party data, prompt injection, and oversized payloads.

Choice & trade-offs

Richer inputs improve user access and require scanning, extraction confidence, provenance, storage policy, and review states. Text-only support is narrower and easier to validate.

QUESTION 266Insurance code & design analysis#

266. Why should fraud or risk signals not be treated as automatic truth?

Read / reveal answer

A statistical risk score is evidence for investigation, not proof that a claimant is dishonest or that a claim should be denied. Training data, false positives, protected proxies, and changing behavior all affect interpretation.

Mechanism & reason

The current code does not implement a fraud model. A proposed extension belongs behind explicit review and evaluation boundaries, separate from coverage retrieval and status lookup.

Risk & safeguards

Presenting an opaque score as a decision can create harmful unsupported accusations and unreliable automation. It also introduces sensitive-data and model-governance obligations beyond this guide's engineering examples.

Choice & trade-offs

Assisted triage can prioritize workload while requiring calibration, review, and appeal paths. Simple rule-based routing may be more transparent for a small system.

QUESTION 267Insurance code & design analysis#

267. How should deterministic calculations complement generated explanations?

Read / reveal answer

Use reviewed deterministic code for exact arithmetic under explicitly selected policy rules, then let generation explain the result and assumptions. A model should not invent a deductible, round a monetary value arbitrarily, or apply an unverified formula.

Mechanism & reason

The backend stores Numeric amounts but does not implement a general payout calculator. The worked example demonstrates Decimal arithmetic as an educational calculation, not an actual coverage or payout rule.

Risk & safeguards

Correct arithmetic with the wrong rule or policy version is still wrong. Caps, sublimits, depreciation, multiple currencies, and exceptions require domain-approved logic.

Choice & trade-offs

Deterministic calculators improve reproducibility and need versioned business specifications. Free-form arithmetic is convenient and unsuitable as authoritative financial logic.

QUESTION 268Insurance code & design analysis#

268. What should a real human-escalation record contain?

Read / reveal answer

Store the originating operation, authorized user or case, reason, minimal evidence, destination, delivery state, and an acknowledgement or failure. Distinguish requested, accepted, in progress, and completed rather than claiming connection from generated prose alone.

Mechanism & reason

The current prompt contains escalation language, but no default registry handler demonstrates specialist assignment. Notification infrastructure is a useful building block, not proof that a human receives a case.

Risk & safeguards

Exporting a full conversation can disclose unnecessary personal data, and a failed handoff can leave a user waiting without an actual owner.

Choice & trade-offs

Structured escalation adds integration work and truthful operational status. A clear support-contact instruction is a legitimate simpler fallback.

QUESTION 269Insurance code & design analysis#

269. How should fairness and accessibility be evaluated in insurance support?

Read / reveal answer

Evaluate whether the assistant handles equivalent requests consistently across language, writing style, disability-related interaction needs, and differing levels of domain knowledge. Focus on support outcomes and access, not unsupported automated judgments about protected traits.

Mechanism & reason

The current prompt and evaluation dataset provide a starting point, but fairness and accessibility require reviewed cases and a usable interface. A high English aggregate score does not establish equivalent performance for other users.

Risk & safeguards

Simplified wording can accidentally omit a condition, while multilingual retrieval can fail under ASCII lexical fallback. Voice or OCR inputs introduce another representation gap.

Choice & trade-offs

Broader evaluation and interface support cost effort and improve real usability. Clearly bounded supported languages are preferable to an untested universal claim.

QUESTION 270Insurance code & design analysis#

270. How should the application explain uncertainty without hiding system failure?

Read / reveal answer

Explain whether facts are missing, evidence is absent, the source is inapplicable, or a backend is unavailable. Give the strongest supported statement while preserving a distinct failure status. Uncertainty should guide the next action rather than be a vague disclaimer appended to everything.

Mechanism & reason

The prompt has an uncertainty ladder, but policy retrieval can convert errors to an empty result and the policy tool then returns a no-information message. That integration loses an important failure distinction from the library's richer types.

Risk & safeguards

An outage can be mistaken for proof that the policy says nothing. Reassuring language may also imply a specialist handoff that never happened.

Choice & trade-offs

Explicit statuses require API and UI changes but make retries and support decisions honest. Text-only fallback is simple and ambiguous.
