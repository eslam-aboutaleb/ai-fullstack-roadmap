QUESTION 001Code & engineering analysis#

1. What is RAG, and where does Ragnor fit in the complete journey?

Read / reveal answer

Retrieval-Augmented Generation combines externally retrieved evidence with a language model's generation. A user question becomes a search request; relevant passages become context; a generator writes an answer conditioned on that context. Ragnor implements reusable ingestion, retrieval, evidence, and operational pieces. A host still supplies the conversational application and its answer-generation policy.

Mechanism & reason

Separate the offline path, which builds a searchable corpus, from the online path, which retrieves evidence for a question. This lets a source change trigger indexing without retraining the language model. The original RAG paper combines parametric model memory with a retrieved non-parametric index; this repository is an engineering subsystem, not a reproduction of that training architecture.

Risk & safeguards

Retrieval can miss the right passage, and a generator can ignore a correct passage. The evidence assembler exposes explicit failure states and citations, but cannot prove every generated claim is true.

Choice & trade-offs

External knowledge improves freshness and traceability at the cost of ingestion, search latency, access control, and evaluation work. Small stable tasks may need only a prompt or structured database query.

Theory reference: Original RAG paper. The project connection below is an engineering interpretation.

QUESTION 002Code & engineering analysis#

2. Why can an LLM sound confident while being wrong?

Read / reveal answer

A Large Language Model predicts token sequences using learned patterns. Fluent continuation and factual verification are different capabilities. If the prompt lacks the relevant fact, or includes ambiguous evidence, a plausible continuation can still be false. Confidence in the prose is not a calibrated probability that the claim is correct.

Mechanism & reason

In this project, the retrieval and evidence layers supply inspectable material before a host invokes generation. EvidenceStatus distinguishes missing evidence from a failed backend, giving the application a basis for abstaining or asking a follow-up question.

Risk & safeguards

Presenting a citation does not establish that the citation supports the attached sentence. Ragnor preserves evidence identity, while claim-to-evidence checking and appropriate abstention remain host responsibilities.

Choice & trade-offs

Restricting answers to supplied evidence improves auditability but can reduce coverage. Allowing general model knowledge expands coverage while weakening the promise that every answer reflects the indexed corpus.

QUESTION 003Code & engineering analysis#

3. How are search, retrieval, and generation different responsibilities?

Read / reveal answer

Search ranks records against a query. Retrieval includes query preparation, backend selection, constraint enforcement, candidate fetching, and optional reranking. Generation turns the resulting evidence and instructions into a new answer. Treating these as one function hides whether an error came from missing data, ranking, or answer synthesis.

Mechanism & reason

HybridRetriever and the strategy classes orchestrate search; EvidenceAssembler builds a bounded evidence bundle. Their return types are useful without a chat model. This separation also supports source verification tools and ordinary search interfaces.

Risk & safeguards

A fallback that silently generates when search fails can convert an infrastructure outage into misinformation. Use strict retrieval and propagate backend failure into the host's response state.

Choice & trade-offs

Explicit stages require more wiring and more status handling. They also let you benchmark retrieval independently and switch generation providers without rebuilding the corpus.

QUESTION 004Code & engineering analysis#

4. When should I use RAG instead of fine-tuning or a SQL query?

Read / reveal answer

Use retrieval when answers depend on a changing collection and need source attribution. Use a structured query when the task is exact aggregation over authoritative fields. Consider fine-tuning when the persistent problem is behavior, style, or task adaptation rather than missing up-to-date facts. These approaches can be combined.

Mechanism & reason

Ragnor's document and evidence abstractions suit passage retrieval. A question such as total approved invoices this month should usually reach a database computation; nearest passages cannot guarantee an exact total. Fine-tuning is not implemented by this subsystem.

Risk & safeguards

A semantic similarity score can make an approximate answer look exact. The host must route tasks appropriately and preserve authorization on both document search and structured tools.

Choice & trade-offs

Task routing adds complexity but avoids forcing every question through a vector index. A single retrieval path is easier to prototype and easier to misuse.

QUESTION 005Code & engineering analysis#

5. What is the difference between a document, a chunk, a vector, and evidence?

Read / reveal answer

A document is a source-level unit of text and metadata. A chunk is a searchable subdivision. An embedding vector is a numerical representation of that subdivision. Evidence is selected, attributed text prepared for a consuming model or user. These objects have different lifetimes and should not share one overloaded identity field.

Mechanism & reason

The canonical Chunk, Document, and SearchResult dataclasses separate ingestion and search shapes; evidence types add revision, source, span, and citation information. A chunk hit becomes useful evidence only after selection and attribution.

Risk & safeguards

Confusing a chunk ID with a document ID breaks deletion, citation lookup, and version switching. Ragnor carries metadata through the pipeline, but hosts must supply stable source identities and meaningful provenance.

Choice & trade-offs

More explicit types require conversion at boundaries. They reduce accidental field loss and make changes to storage independent of the user-facing citation contract.

QUESTION 006Code & engineering analysis#

6. What do dense and sparse retrieval mean?

Read / reveal answer

Dense retrieval compares learned vectors, making semantic paraphrases discoverable. Sparse or lexical retrieval scores token-based signals, making exact names, identifiers, and unusual terms discoverable. They capture different notions of relevance. Neither should be described as understanding the user's authorization or guaranteeing truth.

Mechanism & reason

Ragnor separates VectorIndex and LexicalIndex capabilities and composes them in HybridRetrieval. PostgreSQL offers native full-text search; some other adapters approximate lexical ranking with token overlap. Those are different implementations, not equivalent language models.

Risk & safeguards

Dense retrieval can confuse related concepts; lexical retrieval can miss synonyms. Hybrid fusion reduces dependence on one signal but adds its own ranking choices and cannot recover absent source material.

Choice & trade-offs

Two signals increase implementation and evaluation cost. A keyword-only strategy can be preferable for exact support codes or a prototype with no embedding service.

7. What does grounding actually guarantee?

Read / reveal answer

Grounding means giving an answer process explicit evidence it can use. It is a relationship between a claim and supporting material, not a property conferred by vector search alone. A grounded workflow must preserve provenance, show the relevant revision, and handle insufficient evidence honestly.

Mechanism & reason

EvidenceAssembler deduplicates, merges eligible spans, budgets rendered context, and creates citation labels. SourceLookup can retrieve a bounded span from a retained revision. These mechanisms make verification possible rather than automatically performing every verification step.

Risk & safeguards

A wrong retrieved passage, stale document, or unsupported inference can survive into the answer. Host-side citation checks and evaluation are still necessary; delimiter escaping is only one mitigation against untrusted source instructions.

Choice & trade-offs

Strong attribution needs snapshot retention and richer metadata. Minimal text-only search is cheaper but cannot support reliable historical verification.

QUESTION 008Code & engineering analysis#

8. How do relevance, correctness, faithfulness, and freshness differ?

Read / reveal answer

Relevance asks whether material addresses the question. Correctness asks whether the claim is true. Faithfulness asks whether the answer follows the provided evidence. Freshness asks whether the evidence reflects an appropriate time or revision. An answer can be faithful to an obsolete policy and therefore be incorrect for today's user.

Mechanism & reason

The project separates retrieval evaluation from answer evaluation and carries freshness and revision fields in evidence. These signals allow a host to assess different failure modes rather than collapse quality into a single score.

Risk & safeguards

High retrieval recall does not establish answer correctness. A heuristic overlap score also cannot establish that a reasoning step is valid or that the source itself is authoritative.

Choice & trade-offs

Multiple metrics complicate release decisions but expose regressions that one average hides. The right acceptance criteria depend on the product's questions and corpus.

QUESTION 009Code & engineering analysis#

9. What are the main cost and latency stages in a RAG request?

Read / reveal answer

Typical stages are query transformation, query embedding, candidate search, fusion, reranking, evidence assembly, and host generation. Ingestion has separate extraction, chunking, embedding, and storage costs. The relative expense depends on providers, data size, network placement, and configuration; this repository does not establish universal timings.

Mechanism & reason

Separating candidate_k from final_k permits a larger search pool without necessarily sending every hit to generation. Telemetry records stage events and durations, and the embedding executor bounds concurrency and provider call duration when configured.

Risk & safeguards

Increasing query variants, candidate counts, and retries together can multiply load. No single semaphore controls an entire fleet or gives an end-to-end request deadline.

Choice & trade-offs

Higher candidate recall often costs more latency and money. Optimize against a quality target using measured stage distributions, not guessed millisecond claims.

QUESTION 010Code & engineering analysis#

10. What does domain-agnostic mean, and what does it not solve?

Read / reveal answer

Domain-agnostic means the core can work with different application schemas, sources, settings, and storage implementations through explicit interfaces. It does not mean every domain has identical chunking needs, permissions, legal retention rules, or quality thresholds. Those policies remain part of the host's product design.

Mechanism & reason

Ragnor receives settings, sessions, version storage, embedders, and retrieval strategies rather than importing a host's models. The same core can serve a policy corpus or a support corpus while their adapters and evaluation datasets differ.

Risk & safeguards

Generic abstractions can hide important domain constraints if the host never models them. A tenant filter is useful only when trusted identity selects the correct scope and every storage path respects it.

Choice & trade-offs

Reusability reduces coupling but requires deliberate integration work. A specialized application may be quicker initially and harder to reuse safely later.
