## context-engineering|Context engineering|Build the information environment that makes the next model action reliable.
How is context engineering different from writing a prompt? || Context engineering manages the whole information presented at a decision: instructions, user intent, retrieved evidence, memory, tool schemas, tool results, and output constraints. A prompt is one part of that environment. || Build a context manifest before each model call. Record which source supplied each block, its trust level, purpose, expiry, and token cost. This makes a missing fact distinguishable from a poor instruction. || Mixing user documents with privileged instructions creates an injection path. Keep explicit boundaries and authorize actions outside the model. || More context can improve coverage while increasing cost, distraction, and disclosure. || Design a manifest for a help-desk turn and justify every block.
What belongs in a context contract? || Define required inputs, optional evidence, trusted identities, freshness requirements, allowed tools, output schema, and an abstention policy. The contract describes the information needed to complete one bounded task. || Validate the contract before inference: a missing customer identifier should trigger an authenticated lookup or clarification rather than a guessed record. Keep the server identity out of editable user fields. || A syntactically complete context may still contain stale or contradictory facts. Attach version and applicability metadata. || Strict prerequisites reduce unsupported answers but add clarification turns. || Write contracts for factual search, refund preparation, and approved refund execution.
How should instructions, evidence, and tool output be separated? || Give each source an explicit role and purpose. Instructions describe behavior; evidence supports factual statements; tool output reports an operation result. A retrieved paragraph never gains authority to change permissions. || Use structured envelopes with origin and trust labels. Present source text as quoted data and tell the model how to use it, while enforcing tool restrictions in backend policy. || Delimiters help readability but do not form a security boundary. Malicious evidence can still influence model choices. || Rich envelopes improve traceability and consume tokens. || Inject an instruction into a retrieved article and verify that no unauthorized tool becomes callable.
Why does context need provenance and freshness? || A fact is useful only when its source, applicable time, and revision are known. A current-looking article may describe a retired product or another customer's entitlement. || Carry document ID, revision, retrieval timestamp, effective interval, and access scope through evidence assembly. Recheck authorization when a user opens the cited source. || A summary that omits the effective date can turn accurate historical evidence into an incorrect present-tense answer. || Fine-grained provenance increases storage and citation complexity. || Compare answers for an event occurring before and after a knowledge-rule change.
What is working memory versus long-term memory? || Working memory supports the active task: selected facts, unfinished steps, and recent tool outcomes. Long-term memory persists reviewed preferences or facts across sessions with explicit retention and correction rules. || Write durable memory only after validation. Store its origin, owner, confidence, expiry, and deletion policy; retrieve only entries relevant to the current task. || A mistaken inference can persist and repeatedly bias future answers. Never store speculative sensitive traits as established facts. || More memory reduces repeated questions but adds privacy and consistency obligations. || Design a user-visible correction and deletion workflow for a remembered preference.
How should tool schemas enter context? || Present only tools relevant to the task and allowed for the authenticated principal. Tool names and descriptions should explain purpose, required inputs, side effects, and failure states. || Filter the server registry before model exposure. A model may select among permitted actions, but the backend validates its arguments and permissions again at execution. || Dynamically discovered tool descriptions can contain hostile instructions. Treat remote metadata as untrusted. || A small catalog improves selection efficiency while excessive filtering can hide a needed capability. || Reduce a 100-tool catalog to five tools for one support journey and evaluate missed actions.
How do you handle contradictory evidence? || Identify the conflict instead of averaging incompatible facts. Prefer the correct authority and revision only when an explicit product rule establishes precedence; otherwise ask for review or present uncertainty. || Group passages by subject and applicable interval, compare relevant statements, and surface conflicting source identifiers in the answer contract. || Similarity rank does not establish authority. A highly ranked outdated article can be less applicable than a lower ranked official rule. || Conflict detection costs processing and may reduce answer coverage. || Build a three-document fixture with one outdated and two conflicting current statements.
What should an agent retain after a tool call? || Retain the operation identifier, outcome, essential evidence, next permitted actions, and any durable state change. Large raw payloads can move to a scoped artifact store referenced by ID. || Summarize tool results into a typed observation without dropping error category or confirmation status. The next model call must know whether an action completed. || Replacing a failed operation with a vague success-like summary can cause false completion claims. || Compact observations save tokens but can hide details needed for diagnosis. || Specify a result envelope for a failed payment and a successfully prepared payment.
How is context quality evaluated? || Measure whether necessary facts are present, unauthorized material is absent, sources are applicable, and the model can complete the intended task within budget. Evaluate the assembled context separately from the final answer. || Use known-answer fixtures with evidence labels and adversarial distractors. Compare answer quality while varying one assembly policy at a time. || A context policy can look effective on easy examples while failing on permissions or long conversations. || More realistic fixtures are expensive to curate and reveal failures earlier. || Create ten missing-evidence cases and ten irrelevant-context cases before changing your assembler.
When should an agent ask a question rather than retrieve more context? || Ask when the missing information belongs to the user or cannot be safely inferred, such as which account they mean or what action they intend. Retrieve when an authorized system can supply the missing fact. || Classify the gap as intent, identity, evidence, or system state. Route to clarification, authentication, retrieval, or an operation lookup accordingly. || Repeated retrieval cannot resolve ambiguous user intent and may disclose unrelated data. || Clarification slows completion while reducing costly incorrect actions. || For five ambiguous requests, identify the smallest question that changes the next valid action.
## context-optimization|Context optimization and memory budgets|Measure, select, compress, and cache context without losing critical evidence.
How do you calculate a safe context budget? || Reserve space for instructions, tool schemas, conversation, retrieved evidence, and expected output before adding optional material. Use the provider's tokenizer or conservative model-specific accounting. || Compute remaining input allowance from the model limit and output reservation, then apply per-section caps. Multimodal inputs and hidden protocol overhead may require separate estimates. || A character-based guess can exceed the limit or remove important content unpredictably. || Large reservations reduce available evidence but improve completion reliability. || Calculate budgets for two models with different context windows and output limits.
How should evidence be selected under a token budget? || Select passages for relevance, novelty, applicability, and source coverage rather than filling the window with the first ranked hits. A second near-duplicate passage often adds less value than a missing condition. || Deduplicate by document span, group related chunks, and reserve room for contradictory or qualifying passages. Track why a candidate was included or dropped. || Greedy selection can omit a long decisive clause. Evaluate answerable cases that require multiple passages. || Sophisticated selection adds latency; simple baselines remain useful. || Compare top-k selection with diversity-aware selection on a fixture containing repeated FAQs.
What is the difference between truncation and compression? || Truncation discards material according to a rule; compression rewrites or extracts a smaller representation. Compression can preserve more useful information, but generated summaries can introduce errors. || Prefer extractive spans for authoritative numbers and conditions. If a model summarizes, retain the raw source and evaluate factual preservation before using the summary. || A summary may drop a negation, exception, or unit while remaining fluent. || Compression uses extra inference and can be reused across turns. || Compress a rule with an exception and verify every condition against the original.
How should conversation compaction work? || Preserve the task goal, confirmed facts, unresolved questions, operation IDs, authorization boundaries, and recent state changes. Keep recent turns verbatim when their exact wording affects intent. || Generate a structured checkpoint, validate required fields, then continue with the checkpoint plus recent messages. Durable actions must be read from backend state. || A summary is not a substitute for an audit log or a completed transaction record. || Frequent compaction saves context but introduces recurring summarization error. || Resume a long conversation from a checkpoint and check whether any completed action is repeated.
What is lost-in-the-middle behavior? || Models may use evidence differently depending on where it appears in a long context. The existence and size of the effect depend on the model, task, and data. || Evaluate important evidence placed at the beginning, middle, and end, with realistic distractors. Adjust context structure only after observing the failure. || Treating a published positional effect as a universal ordering rule can worsen a different workload. || Reordering is cheap but cannot recover missing evidence. || Rotate one decisive passage through a long fixture and measure supported-answer rate.
How do prompt caching and semantic caching differ? || Prompt caching reuses computation for supported repeated input prefixes. Semantic caching reuses an earlier application answer for a sufficiently similar request under a defined validity policy. || Keep cache keys tied to tenant, policy, model configuration, evidence version, and required freshness where relevant. Inspect provider caching rules separately from application response reuse. || A semantically similar question from another user can require a different answer or authorization scope. || Reuse saves latency and cost while increasing invalidation complexity. || Design cache keys and invalidation tests for two users with different entitlements.
How should memory retrieval avoid overwhelming the prompt? || Retrieve a small set of task-relevant memory entries using ownership, recency, applicability, and confidence constraints. Long-term memory should remain selective. || Separate user-declared preferences from inferred observations and verified system facts. Rank within the authorized scope, then apply a token budget and expiry checks. || Stale inferred preferences can override a new explicit user request if presented as privileged context. || Narrow retrieval reduces noise but may repeat questions when recall misses a useful fact. || Introduce an expired preference and verify that the current user instruction wins.
When should you compress tool results instead of retrieving fewer records? || Compress when the selected records are necessary but their representation is verbose. Retrieve fewer records when the extras do not contribute to the task. These solve different causes of context growth. || Keep authoritative identifiers and outcome fields, remove repeated schema descriptions, and store detailed payloads behind access-controlled artifact references. || Dropping row-level provenance can make aggregates impossible to verify. || Compact schemas improve efficiency while requiring schema version discipline. || Replace a verbose 50-record tool response with an aggregate and scoped drill-down IDs.
What is token efficiency, and how do you avoid optimizing the wrong metric? || Token efficiency is useful work per cost under a quality and safety constraint. Reducing input tokens while increasing failed turns is not a product improvement. || Compare total task cost, completion rate, supported-answer rate, p95 latency, and repeat calls. Count summarization and cache misses in the same experiment. || A cheaper model call can produce more retries and a more expensive complete journey. || Multi-metric evaluation complicates ranking alternatives but reflects real users. || Run a paired comparison where both context policies face the same task fixtures.
How should context policies be rolled out? || Version the assembly policy, evaluate it offline, compare it on a limited traffic slice, and retain rollback. Include permission and long-conversation cases in release gates. || Record the selected policy version in traces and experiment outputs. Change one dominant factor at a time so regressions can be attributed. || A silent tokenizer or model revision change can invalidate an old budget assumption. || Controlled rollout delays broad savings while reducing unknown failure exposure. || Write a rollout plan with a regression threshold and an explicit rollback trigger.
## prompt-foundations|Prompt engineering foundations|Specify tasks, evidence, output contracts, and uncertainty clearly.
What makes a useful system instruction? || A useful instruction defines the assistant's purpose, allowed capabilities, evidence requirements, interaction style, and escalation behavior. It should make decision rules observable rather than merely assigning a persona. || Separate stable product policy from task-specific instructions and user-provided data. Version the instruction alongside tool schemas and evaluation fixtures. || Long conflicting instructions can obscure the rules that matter most. || Concise rules are easier to maintain while examples may be needed for ambiguous behavior. || Rewrite a vague helpful-assistant prompt into five testable behavior requirements.
How do role, task, context, and output specification work together? || The role supplies the operating perspective, the task defines the work, context supplies relevant facts, and the output contract defines the required result. None should imply capabilities the backend lacks. || Use explicit sections and a small validated schema for structured answers. Include what to do when required facts are missing. || A detailed role description can encourage unjustified confidence without improving evidence. || Structured outputs simplify integration and reduce free-form flexibility. || Design an extraction prompt that returns unknown rather than inventing a missing date.
When are zero-shot and few-shot prompts appropriate? || Zero-shot prompts rely on instructions alone. Few-shot prompts add examples showing the desired mapping, especially useful when labels or output conventions are ambiguous. || Include diverse normal, boundary, and abstention examples. Keep test cases separate so reported performance does not merely reflect prompt memorization. || Biased examples can teach spurious formatting or demographic associations. || Examples improve clarity but consume context and require maintenance. || Compare a classification task with no examples and four balanced examples on a held-out set.
How do negative instructions compare with positive alternatives? || A prohibition is easier to follow when paired with an allowed replacement behavior. For example, require a clarification when a required field is absent. || Turn each major restriction into a condition and action: if evidence is insufficient, state the gap and ask the smallest useful question. || Repeating long lists of forbidden content can distract from the actual task and still cannot enforce permissions. || Explicit alternatives add length while making behavior easier to evaluate. || Replace five vague do-not rules with observable conditional actions.
How should a prompt express uncertainty? || Require the assistant to distinguish supported facts, assumptions, unresolved conflicts, and missing information. Confidence should follow an evaluated policy rather than an adjective chosen by the model. || Attach source-backed statements to evidence IDs and use typed unresolved reasons for downstream handling. || Numerical self-reported confidence is not automatically calibrated. || More abstention can reduce harmful errors while increasing follow-up effort. || Create cases that require a direct answer, a qualified answer, and an abstention.
Why is output parsing a backend responsibility? || The model produces untrusted output even when a schema is requested. The backend must validate structure, types, allowed values, and business invariants before downstream use. || Parse with a schema validator and apply separate domain checks. Reject extra properties where they create ambiguity, and never execute returned code by default. || Schema-valid output can still name an unauthorized resource or unsupported amount. || Strict validation causes recoverable failures instead of silently accepting malformed data. || Send a schema-valid but unauthorized record ID through your proposed handler.
How should prompt templates avoid accidental instruction injection? || Keep trusted instructions and variable data separate. Treat documents, user messages, and tool descriptions as data that may contain adversarial instructions. || Render variables through a controlled template, label sources, and enforce action permissions in backend policy regardless of model output. || Escaping characters and adding delimiters do not guarantee resistance to semantic injection. || Layered controls take more work than a prompt-only defense and provide stronger boundaries. || Insert a malicious instruction into a document field and inspect the resulting tool permissions.
What should be versioned with a prompt? || Version the instruction, examples, tool descriptions, output schema, retrieval policy, model settings, and evaluation dataset references. An instruction string alone does not identify system behavior. || Store a release manifest and attach its ID to traces and evaluation runs. Use immutable artifacts for comparisons. || Changing the tool schema while retaining an old prompt version makes regressions difficult to explain. || Detailed manifests add bookkeeping and enable reproducibility. || Create two releases that differ only in one instruction and compare matched tasks.
What is the right way to debug a poor answer? || Inspect the task contract, evidence, tool trajectory, model output, and parser outcome in order. A bad answer may arise before the prompt ever reaches the model. || Reproduce the failure with captured non-sensitive fixtures. Change the smallest identified cause and add a regression example. || Editing instructions for a retrieval bug can produce a brittle prompt that hides the real problem. || Focused fixes are slower initially than ad hoc tweaking and accumulate more reliable behavior. || Classify five failures as missing context, wrong tool, unsupported generation, or parser rejection.
How do you evaluate prompt quality beyond personal preference? || Define task-specific success criteria and compare prompts on the same held-out examples. Evaluate correctness, groundedness, unsafe actions, latency, and user effort separately. || Blind reviewers to prompt variant, calibrate any model judge against humans, and report subgroup and failure-category results. || A fluent response can win a style preference while failing a business requirement. || Human review costs time but anchors automated judging. || Build a rubric that scores both factual support and whether the correct next action was taken.
## prompt-techniques|Prompt techniques and structured reasoning|Choose decompositions and verification methods by measured task need.
When should a task be decomposed into smaller prompts? || Decompose when subtasks have different evidence, validation rules, or failure recovery. Extraction followed by validation and drafting is often easier to inspect than a single opaque request. || Pass typed intermediate results, not unbounded prose. Validate each boundary before invoking the next stage. || Errors in an early stage can become authoritative inputs to later stages. || Decomposition improves diagnosis and adds latency and orchestration cost. || Split a support response into intent extraction, authorized evidence lookup, and answer generation.
What is chain-of-thought prompting useful for, and what should users see? || Some reasoning tasks benefit from structured intermediate work, but private internal reasoning is not a reliable audit record. Ask for concise conclusions, checkable calculations, evidence, or a plan when useful. || Verify the observable result using tools or deterministic checks. Use public explanations that cite the facts needed to assess the conclusion. || A persuasive reasoning narrative can rationalize an incorrect result. || Additional reasoning can improve some tasks while increasing cost and latency. || Compare direct answers with a short checkable calculation and validate both against known outcomes.
How does self-consistency work? || Generate multiple candidate solutions and aggregate their observable results under a defined rule. Agreement can improve some tasks but does not establish truth when samples share a bias. || Normalize answers before aggregation and track the extra model cost. Require external evidence for factual questions. || Five matching hallucinations are still wrong. || Sampling increases compute and may be unnecessary for deterministic tool-backed tasks. || Compare single-sample accuracy with majority voting on a bounded reasoning dataset.
What is retrieval-grounded prompting? || Supply task-relevant evidence and require answers to distinguish supported statements from unresolved gaps. The retrieval and citation contracts matter as much as the instruction. || Include source IDs, revisions, and applicability notes, then validate citations and critical facts after generation. || A model can copy an irrelevant passage accurately and still answer the wrong question. || Strong grounding improves traceability while limiting unsupported coverage. || Add one irrelevant but plausible passage and check whether the answer cites the right source.
What is plan-and-execute prompting? || A planner proposes steps; an executor performs permitted actions and records outcomes. The plan is a hypothesis that may need revision when tools reveal new facts. || Validate steps against capability and permission constraints, set a budget, and checkpoint durable progress. || Executing a model's plan without backend checks turns a reasoning error into a system action. || Separate planning improves visibility but adds state and recovery complexity. || Require a plan to pause before an irreversible action and resume after approval.
When is a critique-and-revise loop helpful? || Use it when a critic has a specific rubric, additional evidence, or a stronger verification method. Generic requests to improve the answer often produce stylistic churn. || Bound revision count and require each revision to address an identified defect. Re-evaluate against independent acceptance criteria. || A critic can hallucinate errors or remove a correct qualification. || Additional passes improve some outputs and consume latency and tokens. || Give the critic a citation-support rubric and measure whether factual errors actually decrease.
How should constrained decoding be distinguished from semantic validation? || Constrained decoding can restrict output structure or allowed tokens. Semantic validation checks whether the selected values are true, authorized, and meaningful for the application. || Use both when integrating model output with software. Apply ownership checks, numeric constraints, and state-transition rules after parsing. || A perfectly valid JSON object can still request an impermissible action. || Strong output constraints simplify parsing and may limit model expressiveness. || Produce valid JSON containing a nonexistent resource and ensure the domain layer rejects it.
What is prompt chaining versus tool use? || Prompt chaining connects model calls through application-controlled stages. Tool use lets a model request an external capability under host control. A workflow may combine both. || Keep a typed boundary between the proposed tool call and actual execution. Feed the operation outcome back as an observation. || Treating tool-call text as execution proof creates false success. || Chaining is predictable; tool selection is flexible and harder to evaluate exhaustively. || Draw one fixed extraction chain and one agent loop for the same task.
How should prompt injection be evaluated? || Use direct and indirect attacks that attempt data exfiltration, permission escalation, instruction replacement, and false completion. Evaluate resulting actions as well as response wording. || Include attacks inside retrieved documents, OCR text, remote tool metadata, and conversation history. Verify the backend rejects forbidden operations. || Passing a few known jailbreak strings does not establish general resistance. || Adversarial coverage costs maintenance and exposes high-impact gaps. || Add one injection fixture per untrusted input channel and define an invariant for each.
When should you stop optimizing the prompt? || Stop when the dominant failures require better evidence, a reliable tool, changed product scope, or model capability rather than clearer instructions. Optimize the full system against a release target. || Categorize remaining failures and estimate the expected value of the next intervention. Keep a simple baseline for comparison. || Prompt complexity can grow while task reliability remains flat. || Backend improvements take engineering effort but often provide durable guarantees. || Rank your ten largest failures by whether a prompt change could plausibly resolve them.
## mcp-foundations|MCP foundations and capability contracts|Connect agents to tools, resources, and prompts through explicit boundaries.
What is MCP and which problem does it solve? || Model Context Protocol standardizes how compatible hosts and clients discover and interact with server capabilities. It reduces custom integration glue for tools, resources, and reusable prompts. || A host manages model interaction and policy; a client communicates with a server that exposes capabilities. Consult the negotiated protocol revision rather than assuming every server supports the same features. || Interoperability does not imply trust or authorization. || A common protocol helps reuse while adding transport and lifecycle responsibilities. || Map a document-search integration into host, client, server, and underlying service.
How do tools, resources, and prompts differ? || Tools perform requested operations; resources expose addressable context; prompts expose reusable interaction templates. Their selection and use are coordinated by the host. || Keep side-effecting business operations in narrowly scoped tools and use resources for read-oriented artifacts with explicit authorization. || A resource URI can reveal private data if access checks are omitted. || Rich capability modeling improves clarity while increasing implementation work. || Classify a search function, document URI, and review template using these three concepts.
What does capability negotiation mean for an MCP integration? || Participants communicate supported features for a protocol revision so each side can use only compatible behavior. An application must handle absent capabilities explicitly. || Test the actual client and server pair, pin compatible SDK versions, and expose feature-dependent paths only when supported. || Assuming a newer feature exists can break an otherwise healthy integration. || Conservative compatibility reduces features but improves interoperability. || Define a fallback when a server cannot provide a feature your UI would prefer.
How should a tool's input and output contracts be designed? || Use precise names, small input schemas, typed outcomes, and stable identifiers. Describe side effects and missing-input behavior clearly enough for both models and humans. || Validate arguments server-side and return minimal structured data plus a useful explanation. Keep protocol errors distinct from normal business failures. || Descriptions and annotations are metadata, not a permission system. || Rich output helps agents reason while consuming context. || Design a search tool that distinguishes zero matches from backend failure.
How does MCP differ from A2A and REST? || REST commonly exposes application resources and operations. MCP standardizes AI-facing capability access. A2A coordinates tasks between agent systems. They can wrap the same backend while serving different contracts. || Choose the smallest boundary that matches the integration. A deterministic lookup rarely needs a remote agent negotiation layer. || Adding protocols can obscure which service owns authorization and durable writes. || Standardization helps interoperability and adds lifecycle overhead. || Explain why your account-balance lookup uses a tool while a specialist investigation may use a remote task.
Which transport should an MCP server use? || Local stdio suits supervised local processes; remote HTTP transport suits network services with explicit identity and deployment controls. Supported details depend on the protocol and SDK versions. || Choose based on process ownership, trust boundary, network reachability, and operational needs. Implement the lifecycle required by the selected revision. || Never expose a local development server broadly merely because it starts successfully. || Local processes simplify access while remote services enable shared operations and require stronger controls. || Compare a developer desktop connector with a shared enterprise document connector.
Why are remote tool descriptions a trust boundary? || A server can supply text that the host places in the model's context. Hostile metadata can try to redirect behavior before any tool is called. || Approve servers, scope available tools, review metadata changes, and keep secrets outside model-visible descriptions. Backend authorization must still apply on execution. || A read-only annotation cannot prove that a remote implementation has no side effects. || Server review slows onboarding and reduces uncontrolled capability exposure. || Create a tool description containing a malicious instruction and verify host policy remains effective.
How should MCP authorization map to backend identity? || Authenticate the request and map permitted scopes to an application principal. The server must authorize the requested resource and operation using trusted identity. || Validate token issuer, audience, expiry, and required scopes according to the selected authorization profile. Do not pass arbitrary caller tokens through to unrelated services. || A valid token for one resource may be invalid for another. || Fine-grained scopes require lifecycle management and reduce blast radius. || Write allow and deny cases for two tenants using the same tool name.
What should cancellation and timeouts mean? || Cancellation stops work where possible and communicates the resulting operation state. It does not reverse an already committed side effect. || Propagate deadlines, retain operation identifiers, and provide a status lookup for uncertain outcomes. Follow the protocol revision's cancellation behavior. || A client disconnect can leave a server operation running. || Durable status adds storage while supporting safe recovery. || Disconnect during a simulated write and determine whether retry would duplicate it.
What is a good first MCP project? || Build a read-only knowledge-search server with synthetic documents, an authenticated service adapter, structured results, and clear empty versus failure outcomes. || Start with one tool, test compatible clients, and add access-control and injection cases before write tools. Keep provider secrets in server configuration. || A toy search demo is not proof of production readiness. || Narrow scope makes the protocol learnable before distributed security complexity. || Deliver a client transcript, authorization matrix, and failure-recovery demonstration.
## mcp-server-engineering|Build and secure an MCP server|Engineer an adapter with scoped tools, observable outcomes, and deployable contracts.
How should an MCP server be layered? || Keep transport handling, tool schemas, application services, and persistence adapters separate. The MCP handler should translate requests rather than embed the entire business workflow. || Inject a service with a narrow interface and derive the principal from request authentication. Reuse the same application service from HTTP and MCP adapters. || Duplicated business logic can enforce different permissions through different protocols. || Layering adds interfaces while keeping behavior consistent. || Implement one search service behind both an HTTP route and an MCP tool.
How do you create a minimal Python MCP tool? || Use a supported official SDK interface, define a typed function, and expose it through a server with an explicitly selected transport. Keep the first example read-only. || The accompanying SDK example uses the v1 FastMCP style and synthetic data. Pin a compatible v1 SDK; evaluate migration separately for another major version. || A minimal decorator example omits production authentication, quotas, and shutdown handling. || SDK convenience reduces boilerplate and introduces version coupling. || Run the example locally with a compatible client and inspect the advertised schema.
How should tool argument validation work? || Validate shape, length, allowed identifiers, numeric bounds, and application invariants before contacting downstream services. Derive tenant and user scope outside model arguments. || Reject arbitrary SQL, paths, or URLs unless the capability is explicitly designed and constrained for them. Use allowlisted operations and parameter binding. || A well-typed string can still contain a dangerous destination or a cross-tenant identifier. || Narrow inputs reduce flexibility and improve control. || Try oversized queries, unexpected properties, and unauthorized resource IDs.
How do you secure a remote MCP deployment? || Apply request authentication, resource authorization, encrypted transport, origin and host validation where relevant, outbound restrictions, quotas, and secret management. || Follow the selected protocol security guidance and deploy behind an explicit ingress policy. Log security outcomes without storing credentials. || A reverse proxy does not automatically validate application-level permissions or block server-side request forgery. || More controls add operational work and reduce exposure. || Threat-model both a malicious client and a compromised downstream API.
How should tool errors be modeled? || Separate transport or protocol failures from expected application outcomes such as not found, permission denied, unavailable, and needs confirmation. Return stable machine-readable categories. || Map internal exceptions to a controlled envelope and preserve a trace or operation ID for support. Do not expose stack traces or query strings containing private data. || Returning an empty list for every failure causes an agent to misreport outages as no matches. || Typed outcomes require client handling and support reliable decisions. || Simulate one normal empty result and one timeout and compare agent behavior.
How should write tools require human confirmation? || Preparation should produce a reviewable operation with exact parameters, owner, expiry, and revision. Execution should validate an approval bound to that operation. || Keep approval verification and the business transaction server-side. A natural-language yes or model-provided approved flag is insufficient. || Reusing approval after parameters change can authorize a different action. || Two-step operations add interaction and reduce accidental changes. || Change the amount after approval and verify execution rejects the stale approval.
How do you control concurrency and retries for tools? || Apply per-tenant quotas, bounded execution concurrency, downstream deadlines, and an idempotency policy for side effects. Retries must depend on outcome and operation semantics. || Keep the same durable operation key across retry attempts, and query status after an ambiguous failure. Avoid holding database locks during remote model calls. || Retrying a timeout can repeat a committed write. || Conservative retry limits may surface more transient failures while protecting correctness. || Inject a timeout immediately after commit and recover through the operation ledger.
What should MCP observability record? || Record tool name, schema version, permitted principal category, latency, outcome, retry count, and trace linkage. Store arguments only under a reviewed privacy policy. || Use an allowlist of safe attributes and attach task or operation IDs across adapters. Separate user-visible outcomes from instrumentation success. || A tool returning a domain failure may still look like a successful function call. || Detailed payload logging improves diagnosis and increases disclosure risk. || Show metrics that distinguish validation rejection, denied access, empty search, and provider outage.
How should MCP contract tests be organized? || Test schema discovery, normal execution, denied access, malformed inputs, cancellation, timeouts, and client-version compatibility. Add backend invariants separately. || Use synthetic data and a sandbox service. Assert what actions occurred, not only what text the agent returned. || Mock-only tests cannot establish behavior of real transport framing or token validation. || End-to-end tests cost more and catch boundary failures unit tests miss. || Publish a small compatibility matrix for the clients and protocol revisions you actually ran.
How should tool changes be released? || Treat schema, semantic, permission, and outcome changes as API changes. Version incompatible contracts and evaluate tool selection after description edits. || Pin the deployed artifact, announce deprecations, retain rollback, and compare tool trajectories before expanding traffic. || Renaming a field can break stored plans or cached tool metadata. || Backward compatibility costs maintenance and prevents abrupt client failure. || Design a migration from one search field to a structured query without silently changing behavior.
## skills-layers|Skills and layered agent capabilities|Package reusable expertise while preserving policy, permissions, and context efficiency.
What is an agent skill? || A skill is a reusable package of task instructions and optional resources or scripts. It tells a compatible agent how to perform a category of work using available capabilities. || Discover concise metadata first, then load detailed instructions when relevant. Keep the skill's purpose, triggers, dependencies, and limits explicit. || Loading a skill does not establish that its instructions are trustworthy or that its scripts are safe. || Packaging expertise reduces repeated prompting while adding version and supply-chain management. || Write a skill for reviewing a support response against an evidence rubric.
How do skills differ from tools and system instructions? || System instructions define product behavior and constraints. Skills supply task-specific methods. Tools provide executable capabilities. A skill can explain tool use without granting tool authority. || Resolve instructions through the actual host's precedence rules and enforce permissions in backend execution. Different products may package skills differently. || A skill that claims to override host policy creates confusion and potential unsafe behavior. || Separate layers improve reuse and require clear ownership. || Draw which layer can propose a refund and which layer can authorize it.
What is progressive disclosure for skills? || Load a small description for routing, the main skill instructions when needed, and supporting references only for the selected task. This avoids inserting every skill into every turn. || Keep the entry document short and link narrowly scoped supporting material. Measure both skill-selection accuracy and context cost. || Vague metadata can select the wrong skill or hide a useful one. || Fine-grained skills save context and increase discovery complexity. || Compare loading ten complete skills with metadata-first discovery on the same tasks.
How should skill layers be organized? || Separate organization-wide rules, application policy, role-specific methods, task skills, and local working context. The host must define actual precedence and conflict behavior. || Keep stable constraints outside editable task packages. Skills should reference reviewed policy rather than duplicate it inconsistently. || An outdated copied policy can silently disagree with a newer application rule. || Centralized policy supports consistency and may reduce team autonomy. || Create a conflict fixture where a task skill requests a prohibited data export.
How should a skill invoke scripts safely? || A skill may describe scripts, but execution requires a controlled runtime, known inputs, constrained filesystem access, and appropriate credentials. Review code and dependencies separately from prose. || Prefer deterministic utilities with narrow arguments and synthetic fixtures. Run untrusted scripts in a sandbox with explicit resource and network limits. || Prompt text cannot make arbitrary shell execution safe. || Sandboxing limits flexibility and contains failures. || Add a script that validates an answer schema without network access or secrets.
How should skills be versioned and tested? || Version the package, referenced resources, scripts, dependencies, and supported tool contracts. Evaluate whether the skill is selected and whether it improves task completion. || Maintain positive, negative, and conflict-trigger examples, plus an end-to-end task suite. Reconnection needs explicit replay and deduplication rules. || Persist critical commands or outcomes with stable IDs and make repeated handling idempotent. || Receiving a frame is not proof that a business transaction committed. || Stronger delivery semantics add storage and protocol complexity. || Disconnect between processing a command and sending its acknowledgment.
How should flow control work for WebSockets? || Bound inbound and outbound queues, limit frame sizes, and define how producers pause or shed nonessential progress. Standard browser WebSocket APIs do not provide automatic application backpressure. || Monitor buffered bytes and processing latency. Prioritize control messages such as cancellation over bulk content. || An audio producer can overwhelm a slow consumer and cause memory growth or large delays. || Dropping stale audio reduces latency and may affect intelligibility. || Simulate a slow network and ensure cancellation still reaches the server promptly.
What are ping, pong, and application heartbeats for? || Transport-level ping and pong help detect connection liveness where exposed. Application heartbeats can verify end-to-end processing and carry session status. || Set timeouts compatible with proxies and mobile networks, and distinguish temporary silence from failure. || A healthy connection does not prove that the agent or downstream service is progressing. || Aggressive timeouts reduce dead sessions and create false disconnects. || Pause the application worker while leaving the socket open and inspect health signals.
How should reconnecting clients recover state? || Reauthenticate, present a resume token or last processed sequence, and receive replay or a state snapshot. The server decides whether the old session can safely resume. || Expire resume capabilities, bind them to the owner, and deduplicate commands and events. || Replaying every outbound client message can repeat a side effect. || Durable recovery costs state storage and supports unstable networks. || Reconnect after an ambiguous write and recover through its operation ID.
How do WebSocket servers scale horizontally? || Maintain durable session and task state outside one process, route active connections deliberately, and distribute events through a shared channel when needed. || Capacity-plan open connections, memory, bandwidth, worker count, and draining behavior. Sticky routing does not replace durable recovery. || A process-local registry loses sessions on restart and cannot coordinate a fleet. || Shared infrastructure adds latency and enables scale and recovery. || Drain one instance during active sessions and verify the expected reconnect path.
How should binary audio and text events coexist? || Define a protocol that identifies audio format, stream, timestamps, and sequence while using typed control events for transcripts and state. Keep ordering assumptions explicit. || Validate sample rate, channels, codec, and payload size. Maintain separate playback and control queues when useful. || Misinterpreted encoding can produce noise, broken timing, or excessive memory use. || A richer protocol adds implementation work and supports multimodal sessions. || Change the declared audio format midstream and verify a controlled rejection.
What should a WebSocket production test include? || Test authorization, origin checks, oversized frames, expired sessions, malformed messages, reconnects, duplicate commands, slow consumers, network loss, and deployment draining. || Measure end-to-end command latency and queue depth under realistic concurrency. || A local echo server does not establish reliability of a distributed agent session. || Load tests require representative infrastructure and reveal connection capacity limits. || Publish a capacity report with connection count, memory use, and p95 control-message latency.
## voice-agents|Voice agents and realtime audio|Build conversational audio with turn control, interruption, and safe actions.
How does a cascaded voice pipeline differ from speech-to-speech? || A cascaded system uses speech recognition, a text-based agent, and speech synthesis. A speech-to-speech model processes and generates audio more directly, with provider-specific capabilities. || Compare transcript control, latency, language support, observability, and tool integration. Evaluate the complete spoken conversation rather than only transcript quality. || Direct audio generation can make textual audit and output filtering harder. || Cascades add stage latency and offer clearer intermediate boundaries. || Diagram both designs for the same support task and identify their dominant delays.
What are ASR, STT, TTS, and VAD? || ASR and STT refer to speech recognition or speech-to-text; TTS converts text to speech; VAD detects speech activity. They address different parts of a voice interaction. || VAD segments activity, recognition produces hypotheses, the agent resolves intent, and synthesis produces playback audio. Track timing and errors at each stage. || VAD can confuse background noise with speech or clip quiet words. || Separate components improve diagnosis and increase integration complexity. || Record a noisy phrase and identify which stage first introduced an error.
How should partial transcripts be handled? || Treat partial recognition as revisable hypotheses. Only finalized or explicitly confirmed content should drive consequential actions. || Attach utterance IDs and revisions to transcript events and replace earlier hypotheses rather than appending them as independent facts. || Acting on an unfinished phrase can invert intent when the user adds a negation. || Waiting for finalization adds latency and improves intent stability. || Stream I want to cancel followed by not the order, the reminder and inspect tool behavior.
What is turn detection and why is it difficult? || Turn detection decides when the user has finished speaking and when the agent should respond. Silence alone may reflect hesitation, language patterns, or network delay. || Combine acoustic activity with configurable timeouts and conversational signals. Let users correct premature interruptions. || Overly aggressive endpointing can cut off names, numbers, or accessibility-related speech patterns. || Longer waits improve completeness and increase perceived delay. || Evaluate turn boundaries across slow speech, pauses, background noise, and multiple languages.
How should barge-in and interruption work? || When the user interrupts, stop or duck playback, cancel obsolete generation where possible, and update conversation state to reflect what was actually delivered. || Use generation IDs to reject stale audio after cancellation. Track played audio duration separately from generated audio. || The agent may assume the user heard content that remained in a playback buffer. || Precise interruption handling adds state and improves conversational control. || Interrupt halfway through a confirmation and verify that no action is authorized by unplayed content.
How should a voice agent confirm sensitive actions? || Present the material parameters in a clear review and obtain a valid confirmation under the product's authorization policy. Recognition confidence alone is insufficient for consequential intent. || Bind confirmation to the exact operation and require a stronger authenticated channel when appropriate. || Background speech, spoofed audio, or transcript errors can trigger false intent. || Stronger confirmation adds friction and protects high-impact operations. || Change an amount after spoken review and verify the old confirmation cannot execute it.
How should audio latency be measured? || Measure capture-to-transcript, end-of-turn-to-first-response-audio, tool delay, synthesis delay, jitter, and playback buffering. User-perceived latency spans all stages. || Correlate monotonic timestamps across client and server where possible, and report p95 delays under realistic networks. || A fast model token metric can hide slow endpoint detection or playback buffering. || More instrumentation increases complexity and identifies the real bottleneck. || Build a timing waterfall for one interrupted spoken conversation.
How do WebRTC and WebSockets differ for voice? || WebRTC provides media-oriented transport and real-time communication features; WebSockets provide a general ordered duplex channel. The best choice depends on client, provider, and infrastructure support. || Evaluate audio encoding, network behavior, media controls, authentication, and deployment complexity. Use short-lived credentials for direct client sessions when supported. || A long-lived provider secret must not be embedded in browser code. || WebRTC can suit interactive media while requiring signaling and session setup. || Explain the credential flow for a browser-to-provider audio connection.
How should voice privacy and accessibility be designed? || Decide whether raw audio, transcripts, and derived summaries are retained, for how long, and who can access them. Provide a usable text alternative and visible recording state. || Minimize collection, redact sensitive content where appropriate, and evaluate speech quality across accents, noise, and assistive needs. || Voice can reveal identity and sensitive traits beyond the spoken words. || Reduced retention limits diagnosis and exposure. || Design a deletion request that covers audio artifacts, transcripts, and searchable derivatives.
How should voice agents be evaluated? || Measure task completion, transcription errors affecting intent, interruption quality, unsafe actions, latency, and accessibility across representative speakers and conditions. || Use consented or synthetic fixtures and human listening review for aspects text metrics miss. Validate durable outcomes in sandbox services. || Word error rate alone cannot measure whether the correct task was completed. || Audio evaluation costs more and captures real interaction failures. || Compare two agents on noisy numeric inputs, corrections, interruptions, and failed tool calls.
## ocr-document-ai|OCR and document intelligence|Turn images and documents into grounded, reviewable information.
How does OCR differ from document understanding? || OCR recognizes text from images. Document understanding also identifies structure, fields, tables, relationships, and meaning needed for a task. Vision-language models may assist but require validation. || Preserve the original artifact and attach page, region, and extraction provenance to text and fields. || Correct characters in the wrong reading order can still yield an incorrect conclusion. || Rich structure improves downstream retrieval and costs more processing. || Compare plain OCR text with a layout-aware extraction of a two-column document.
How should scanned files enter an ingestion pipeline? || Validate file type and size, quarantine untrusted uploads, scan according to policy, extract pages, perform OCR where needed, and index only reviewed derived content. || Keep upload, extraction, indexing, and publication states separate. Use scoped artifact IDs instead of trusting user-supplied filesystem paths. || Malformed documents can exploit parsers or exhaust resources. || Isolation adds latency and protects the ingestion service. || Submit an oversized synthetic PDF and verify rejection occurs before expensive processing.
Why do bounding boxes and page references matter? || They connect recognized text to a location in the original document, enabling source review and visual verification. A plain text answer loses that spatial traceability. || Store page number, normalized coordinates, extraction version, and source revision with relevant spans. || Re-rendering or cropping without updating coordinates can make citations point to the wrong region. || Spatial metadata increases storage and supports reliable review. || Open a cited field in the source viewer and highlight the exact recognized region.
How should tables be extracted? || Preserve row, column, header, merged-cell, unit, and footnote relationships. Flattening table cells into prose can pair a value with the wrong label. || Use structured extraction plus deterministic validation for totals, units, and required headers. Retain page and cell provenance. || A plausible table reconstruction may silently invent missing cells. || Higher-fidelity parsing costs more and improves numerical tasks. || Extract a table containing a footnote that changes one row's meaning.
How should OCR confidence be used? || Treat confidence as an extractor-specific signal, not a universal probability that a business field is correct. Calibrate thresholds against reviewed examples for the target documents. || Combine confidence with format checks, cross-field consistency, and human review for high-impact values. || A confidently recognized wrong digit can still pass a superficial threshold. || More review reduces automation throughput and catches consequential errors. || Build a reviewed dataset of dates and amounts and measure false acceptance at several thresholds.
How can document text inject instructions into an agent? || OCR and vision outputs are untrusted source content. A document can contain instructions aimed at changing the agent's behavior or leaking data. || Label extracted content as evidence, restrict tools independently, and evaluate attacks placed inside visible text, tables, and images. || Converting an image into text does not make the text trusted. || Layered controls add work and keep document content from becoming authority. || Place a harmless malicious-instruction fixture in an image and verify no unauthorized action occurs.
How should multimodal retrieval combine text and images? || Index text, captions, and image representations under a shared document identity while preserving each representation's provenance and access scope. Choose representations based on the task. || Retrieve candidates using suitable models and validate the relevant original region before a consequential conclusion. || A generated caption can omit a decisive visual feature or add unsupported detail. || Multiple representations improve recall and increase cost and synchronization work. || Compare a text-only search with a mixed representation search on diagram-heavy documents.
How should extracted fields be validated? || Apply types, allowed formats, ranges, cross-field consistency, and business rules after extraction. Preserve missing and ambiguous states rather than filling them with guesses. || Separate raw recognized text from normalized values and retain transformations such as date interpretation or currency conversion. || A locale mismatch can swap day and month while producing a valid date. || Strict normalization causes review cases and reduces silent corruption. || Parse ambiguous dates under two locales and require an explicit resolution policy.
How should document revisions and deletions propagate? || Track original and derived artifacts under a revision identity, then invalidate text, embeddings, summaries, caches, and citations when policy requires. || Publish a new index snapshot only after the replacement is complete. Keep retention rules separate from search visibility. || Removing the original file alone leaves private derived data accessible. || Full lineage costs storage and supports coherent lifecycle management. || Delete a synthetic document and verify every derived retrieval path stops exposing it.
How should an OCR system be evaluated end to end? || Measure character or word errors where useful, field accuracy, table structure, citation location, downstream task success, and human-review burden. Segment by document type and scan quality. || Include rotated, blurred, multilingual, handwritten, and adversarial examples that match the product scope. || High OCR accuracy can coexist with poor business-field accuracy. || Broad fixtures require effort and reveal practical coverage limits. || Publish a confusion analysis showing which document types need human review.
## live-agents|Live agents, event-driven work, and realtime state|Keep long-lived assistants responsive, bounded, and recoverable.
What makes an agent live or realtime? || A live agent reacts to ongoing inputs and changing state under a latency target. It may process speech, events, sensors, or user interaction rather than one isolated request. || Define the required response deadline and which state changes can invalidate active work. Use an explicit session and task lifecycle. || Calling a system realtime without a measured latency contract hides important product limitations. || Lower latency can reduce deliberation and increase infrastructure cost. || Specify a latency target for interruption handling separately from final task completion.
How should events trigger agent work? || Validate and normalize external events, deduplicate them, check scope, and schedule bounded tasks. The model should not directly subscribe to an unrestricted event firehose. || Use an event envelope with ID, source, subject, revision, and timestamp. Record why an event created or updated a task. || Duplicate or reordered events can repeat actions or restore stale state. || Event infrastructure adds complexity and supports responsive workflows. || Deliver the same update twice and verify only one task effect occurs.
How do you handle stale work when new events arrive? || Associate work with a state version or generation ID. Before publishing a result or committing a write, verify that the assumptions remain applicable. || Cancel or supersede obsolete generation and use conditional writes for authoritative state. || An old model response can overwrite a newer user instruction if arrival order is treated as authority. || Version checks can discard useful computation and preserve correctness. || Send a correction while the agent is working and reject the earlier draft as stale.
What is event-time versus processing-time reasoning? || Event time describes when something occurred; processing time describes when the system handled it. Delayed delivery makes them different and can change applicability decisions. || Retain both timestamps and define ordering and lateness policies per event type. Use monotonic clocks for local duration measurement. || Sorting only by arrival can misrepresent the user's actual sequence of actions. || Lateness handling adds state and supports unstable networks. || Process a late event and decide whether it updates history, current state, or neither.
How should long-lived session memory be bounded? || Keep a compact working state, recent events, durable operation references, and relevant retrieved memory. Archive detailed history under controlled retention instead of appending forever. || Periodically checkpoint and compact using validated fields. Rebuild essential facts from authoritative systems after restart. || Unbounded histories increase token cost and retain unnecessary private data. || Compaction reduces detail and controls operational growth. || Run a synthetic session through hundreds of events and measure memory and prompt size.
How should human escalation work for live agents? || Create a durable handoff with owner, reason, urgency, safe summary, evidence, completed actions, and unresolved questions. Tell the user the actual handoff state. || Integrate with a real queue or ticket service and expose acceptance and resolution events. || Saying a specialist has been contacted without a recorded handoff is false completion. || Human review adds delay and handles ambiguity beyond automation scope. || Simulate an unavailable escalation service and show an honest fallback.
How do presence and connection state differ from task state? || Presence indicates whether a client is connected or recently active. Task state records accepted work, progress, and outcomes. A disconnected user may still have a running task. || Keep separate connection, session, and task records with distinct expiry and recovery rules. || Tying task lifetime blindly to one socket can lose durable work on a network interruption. || Separate lifecycles add modeling and improve recovery. || Close the client, complete a background task, and reconnect to its result.
How should live agents handle overload? || Use admission control, bounded queues, priority policies, and explicit degraded behavior. Prefer rejecting new work clearly over silently accumulating unbounded delay. || Reserve capacity for cancellation, status, and safety controls. Track queue age and deadline expiry. || Treating every request equally can starve critical controls behind bulk generation. || Priority policies add fairness decisions and keep the system operable. || Flood nonessential events and verify that cancellation remains responsive.
What should live-agent observability emphasize? || Track event lag, queue age, active sessions, stale-result rejection, reconnect rate, cancellation latency, task outcomes, and cost per active period. || Correlate events, tasks, model runs, and operations with stable IDs and safe metadata. || A healthy socket count can conceal a stalled worker or outdated responses. || Fine-grained metrics increase instrumentation effort and reveal real responsiveness. || Create an alert for rising event lag while HTTP health remains green.
How should live-agent reliability be demonstrated? || Show duplicate delivery, out-of-order events, network loss, user interruption, process restart, provider outage, and overload recovery under a declared contract. || Use synthetic scenarios with expected final states and record timing evidence. Review both user-visible behavior and durable effects. || A short low-load demo cannot establish long-running reliability. || Failure demonstrations take time and provide strong engineering evidence. || Record a session that survives a restart without duplicating an approved action.

How should the learning journey be sequenced? || Learn backend and data fundamentals, retrieval and evidence, agent tool use, context and prompts, durable orchestration, evaluation, then multimodal and live delivery. What should a public portfolio say about AI assistance? || Explain your role in design, implementation, review, and validation, including where AI helped.
