LEARNING PATH / 30 STAGES

Learn by tracing.
Prove it by building.

Start with Python, async functions, HTTP, basic SQL, Git, and browser JavaScript. If one is unfamiliar, pause at that seam and build a tiny exercise before continuing.

A realistic study rhythm

Read ten questions, trace code, draw a failure case, complete the lab, and defend a trade-off aloud. The full path can take roughly 20–36 weeks of sustained part-time study depending on your starting point. This is flexible guidance, not a promise of job readiness.

Stages 1–14 develop the Ragnor core. Stages 15–20 connect the insurance host, databases, concurrency, security, and ADK. Stages 21–23 compare LangChain, LangGraph, and A2A. Stages 24–27 cover agent evaluation, tooling, observability, and applicable evidence. Stages 28–30 join browser delivery, production recovery, and model engineering.

For database depth, prioritize 16–19. For agents, follow 20–26 after core retrieval. Complete 27–30 for the capstone and recovery demonstrations.

STAGE 03 · Q21–30

  2. Create complete, incomplete, inaccessible, and authoritative-empty scan scenarios.

STAGE 05 · Q41–50

Stress the embedding boundary

Exercise response validation, retries, cancellation, and process-local concurrency limits.

Build & investigate

  1. Create fake providers returning wrong counts, wrong dimensions, non-finite values, transient failures, and permanent failures.
  2. Use injected sleep and clock to observe backoff without real waiting.
  3. Run concurrent calls and compare one executor with multiple instances.

Acceptance criteria

Malformed vectors cannot reach storage. Permanent failures are not repeatedly retried, cancellation propagates, and you can explain why per-instance limits are not fleet limits.

Solution outline & review guidance

Wrap the fake function in EmbeddingExecutor with a declared profile. Record active calls with try/finally bookkeeping. Validate that failure occurs before upsert, then distinguish admission queue length from in-flight provider count. Do not infer an input-queue bound from the semaphore.

STAGE 10 · Q91–100

Recover from a stale worker

Reproduce a lease-expiry race and show why fencing must cover activation.

Build & investigate

  1. Pause worker A after preparation, expire or revoke its lease, then acquire a newer token for B.
  2. Resume A and inspect whether it can activate or checkpoint.
  3. Leave abandoned vectors and verify manifest-gated reads and later cleanup.

Acceptance criteria

A cannot commit obsolete visibility. Active chunks remain readable, tombstoned or uncommitted chunks are hidden, and duplicate job execution is safe.

Solution outline & review guidance

Use controlled scheduling events and the in-memory stores to understand the history, then require the same contract from durable adapters. Check compare_and_swap_many all-or-nothing behavior. Prepare a crash matrix including failure after manifest commit but before checkpoint or cleanup.

STAGE 11 · Q101–110

Enforce the trust boundary

Build adversarial cases against both search and source lookup.

Build & investigate

  1. Configure a token validator and explicit deny-by-default scope mapping.
  2. Attempt cross-scope search, copied-citation lookup, and management operations as a reader.
  3. Test malicious source instructions, unsafe redirect destinations, and raw error leakage.

Acceptance criteria

Denied callers receive no private text, including through citations, rerankers, or logs. Server-side denial still holds when a simulated model follows the attacker.

Solution outline & review guidance

Separate identity establishment from scope selection. Use the bridge and trusted MCP identity provider; never accept a model-chosen client_id as authentication. Inspect ordinary logs separately from sanitized events, and remember CORS is not authentication.

Graduation review

Demonstrate a permitted query, denied cross-tenant access, unanswerable query, source outage, provider retry, stale-worker rejection, revision lookup, and restart recovery. Present a held-out retrieval and answer-quality report, a cost model, and a rollback plan.

Score each explanation from 0 to 3: 0 cannot explain; 1 defines terms; 2 traces mechanism and failure; 3 proves it with code, an experiment, and a defended alternative. Aim for level 3 on trust boundaries and consistency before claiming production readiness.
